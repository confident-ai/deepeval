The pitch consistency metric checks whether your voice agent **keeps the same voice from one reply to the next**, rather than sounding clearly deeper or higher in one of them. It returns the share of agent replies that stay close to the agent's typical pitch, between 0 and 1.

<AudioSample name="pitch-consistency" />

## Required Arguments

To use the `PitchConsistencyMetric`, you'll have to provide a [`ConversationalTestCase`](/docs/evaluation-multiturn-test-cases) with:

- `audio` on the agent's turns, one clip per reply, for at least three replies

## Usage

The `PitchConsistencyMetric()` can be used for [end-to-end](/docs/evaluation-end-to-end-multi-turn) evaluation of voice calls, such as ones produced by the [`ConversationSimulator` in voice mode](/docs/conversation-simulator-voice-mode):

```python
from deepeval import evaluate
from deepeval.dataset import ConversationalGolden
from deepeval.metrics import PitchConsistencyMetric
from deepeval.simulator import ConversationSimulator
from deepeval.voice import ElevenLabsConnector, VoiceConfig


golden = ConversationalGolden(
    scenario="The user asks the agent to look up an order by its number.",
    expected_outcome="The agent reads back the order status.",
)

simulator = ConversationSimulator(
    voice_config=VoiceConfig(
        connector=ElevenLabsConnector(agent_id="your-agent-id"),
    )
)
test_cases = simulator.simulate([golden])

metric = PitchConsistencyMetric(threshold=0.8, max_shift_semitones=3)
evaluate(test_cases=test_cases, metrics=[metric])
```

There is **ONE** optional parameter specific to the `PitchConsistencyMetric`, on top of the [common voice metric parameters](/docs/metrics-voice-introduction#using-voice-metrics):

- [Optional] `max_shift_semitones`: an integer for how far, in semitones, a reply's base pitch may sit from the agent's typical base pitch. Must be greater than 0, or a `ValueError` is raised. Defaulted to `3`.

### As a standalone

You can also run the `PitchConsistencyMetric` on a single test case as a standalone, one-off execution:

```python
metric.measure(test_cases[0])

print(metric.score, metric.reason)
print(metric.score_breakdown)
```

See [Events](#events) for what `score_breakdown` contains.

Each reply whose pitch drifted too far shows up in `score_breakdown`. Here is what the [example call](#example-call) returns:

```python
            "timing_source": "turn_timestamps",
            "description": "Voice sat 4 semitones lower than in the agent's other replies",
            "pitch_base_hz": 157.3,

            "start_time": 10.0,
            "mark": "whole_reply",

        {
            "type": "pitch_shift",
            "turn": 7,
            "timing_source": "turn_timestamps",
            "description": "Voice sat 5 semitones higher than in the agent's other replies",
            "pitch_base_hz": 262.1,
            "shift_semitones": 4.6,
            "offset_seconds": 0.0,
            "duration_seconds": 6.0,
            "start_time": 26.0,
            "mark": "whole_reply",
        },
    ],
    "skipped": [
        {
            "turn": 11,
            "error": "Reply has under 3s of voiced speech to follow its pitch.",
        }
    ],

```

| Field | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| `pitch_base_hz` | The reply's base pitch, in Hz: the low end its voice keeps returning to |
| `shift_semitones` | How far that sits from the agent's typical base pitch, in semitones. Negative when deeper |

Each event covers the whole reply: `offset_seconds` is 0 and `duration_seconds` is the clip's length. The `description` rounds the shift to a whole number. Replies with under 3 seconds of voiced speech, or audio that can't be decoded, are listed under `skipped`.

Every event also has the [fields all voice events share](/docs/metrics-voice-introduction#events), such as `mark` and `description`.

`eligible_turns` is how many agent replies were compared and `turns_with_events` how many drifted. The score is the share that didn't.

## How Is It Calculated?

The `PitchConsistencyMetric` score is calculated according to the following equation:

<Equation formula="\text{Pitch Consistency} = \frac{\text{Number of Agent Replies Near the Typical Pitch}}{\text{Total Number of Agent Replies}}" />

The metric reads per-turn audio, one agent clip at a time, or replies cut from stereo full call audio when no turn has audio. The caller's turns are never checked.

### Which replies are scored

A reply is scored if it has at least 3 seconds of voiced speech, since its base pitch takes that long to settle. The metric tracks the pitch of every 40 ms of the clip, between 70 and 500 Hz, and leaves out stretches that are near silent or have no clear pitch. In each 40 ms, it takes the shortest repeat period that fits about as well as the best one, since any multiple of the true period fits just as well and would read an octave or more too low.

It takes at least **3** replies to tell which one is off. With fewer, the metric is [skipped](/docs/metrics-voice-introduction#when-a-metric-is-skipped).

### Pitch shift criteria

Each reply is judged on its base pitch: the low end its voice keeps returning to between the words it stresses. The low end moves far less from reply to reply than the middle does, so a reply full of stressed words doesn't look like a different voice.

The agent's typical base pitch is the median across all counted replies. A median means one odd reply is flagged on its own, rather than pulling the typical pitch towards it and making the others look off too.

| Check | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Base pitch | The 10th percentile of the reply's tracked pitch |
| Second reading | The 20th percentile, read the same way, since a few creaky frames can drag the very lowest pitch down on their own |
| Shift | How far each reading sits from the typical one. When both move the same way, the shift is the smaller of the two. Otherwise it is 0 |
| Pitch shift | The shift is more than `max_shift_semitones` (3 by default) above or below |

A few creaky frames move one reading. A changed voice moves both.

:::note
On real calls, a reply's base pitch stays within 3 semitones of the agent's other replies in all but about 1 reply in 1,000.
:::
