Many teams first hear about a quality problem in their LLM app from a user,
because error logs and latency charts catch requests that fail or slow down
but say nothing about whether the answer was correct, relevant, or safe.

LLM observability tools are meant to fill that gap, and they approach it from
different directions. Old observability tools like Datadog and Honeycomb have
added LLM views beside the rest of their infrastructure, whereas newer tools
like Langfuse, LangSmith, and Arize grew up around AI tracing. Confident AI,
LangWatch, and Latitude were initially built around evaluating AI production
traffic but have since extended to observability as well.

This guide compares ten of them for teams shipping AI agents, RAG pipelines,
and conversational agents, based on each vendor's own documentation as of
October 2026.

## What is LLM observability?

**LLM observability** is the practice of capturing what your AI application
did in production, including every LLM call, tool call, retrieval, and agent
step, and using that record to debug failures, monitor quality, and decide what
to improve next.

It's built on [tracing](/docs/evaluation-llm-tracing), where a trace records
one request from start to finish and the spans inside it record each step
along the way, which lets you follow a bad answer back to the step, tool call,
AI decision, or component of the system that caused it.

LLM observability overlaps with a few neighboring categories, so it's
important to be clear about how it differs from traditional APM, an AI
gateway, or an LLM evaluation framework. Traditional APM monitors
infrastructure such as latency, uptime, and error rates, so it can tell you a
request succeeded but not whether the answer was correct. An AI gateway routes
requests across model providers and logs them along the way, which gives you
request-level visibility but little about the steps inside an agent. An
[LLM evaluation framework](/blog/top-5-llm-evaluation-frameworks) such as
DeepEval scores your app in code, usually before launch and in CI, whereas LLM
observability captures and evaluates what your app does with real users in
production.

In our view, the most useful observability tools in 2026 are the ones that
judge the quality of what they record, because once traffic grows, nobody on
the team can read every trace by hand.

## What to look for in an LLM observability tool

Most tools on this list will catch errors and slow requests, so the harder
problem to solve is spotting outputs that are technically valid but actually
wrong, such as a hallucinated response, a wrong claim, a tone that drifts
during a conversation, or a violation of your policies or instructions. All of
these can lead to a bad user experience, so we compared the tools below on how
well they handle them across the following eight criteria:

1. **Tracing granularity:** you should be able to see every step of a
   multi-step agent or RAG pipeline, including tool calls, retrieved
   documents, and intermediate reasoning.
2. **Built-in evaluation:** look for metrics that score production outputs for
   faithfulness, relevance, hallucination, and safety without you writing each
   judge prompt from scratch.
3. **Quality-aware alerting:** a drop in evaluation scores should reach your
   team through the same channels as a latency spike.
4. **Multi-turn monitoring:** many conversational failures only show up a few
   turns in, so the tool should evaluate whole conversations as well as
   individual requests.
5. **Production-to-development loop:** traces that fail in production should
   become test cases for the next release instead of staying in a dashboard.
6. **Collaboration beyond engineering:** PMs, QA, and domain experts often
   know what a good answer looks like better than engineers do, so they need
   a way to review and annotate traces themselves.
7. **Framework flexibility:** OpenTelemetry support keeps your instrumentation
   portable, while tools tied to one framework tend to lose depth outside it.
8. **Deployment and cost:** check whether self-hosting is available on the
   plan you can afford, and how pricing grows with trace volume.

## 1. Confident AI

[Confident AI](https://www.confident-ai.com/products/llm-observability) is an
LLM observability platform whose starting point is that a trace nobody
evaluates doesn't tell you much. It runs evaluations on
traces and spans as they're ingested, and on conversation threads once they
complete, using the same 50+ metrics that DeepEval provides, so quality sits
on the same dashboards as latency and cost.

Alerts fire on threshold rules over traces, spans, and threads, and on
regressions or anomalies that Drift, Confident AI's version-comparison view,
detects in evaluation scores, and they go out through Slack, Teams, PagerDuty,
Discord, or email. Automated tasks can pull matching traces into annotation
queues and assign them to reviewers, who label what went wrong, and Confident
AI's error analysis then groups that feedback into named failure modes with a
suggested metric for each one. Dataset ingestion tasks add matching traces,
spans, or threads to a dataset as goldens (test cases), so the cases your app
sees in production end up in your next evaluation run.

Confident AI is also designed for people outside engineering, so PMs, QA, and
domain experts can work through annotation queues and, once an engineer has
connected your app's HTTPS endpoint, run evaluations against it without
writing code. That way the engineering team doesn't become a bottleneck for
product quality, which PMs, QA, and domain experts are often better placed to
judge.

### Key features

- Online evaluation of traces and spans, plus thread evaluation for
  multi-turn conversations, with 50+ research-backed metrics
- Alerts on threshold rules and on Drift regressions in evaluation scores,
  delivered to Slack, Teams, PagerDuty, Discord, or email
- Signals, which label traces and threads with LLM-driven categories you
  define and surface spikes and trends over time
- Drift detection across quality, reliability, latency, cost, and classifier
  outcomes
- Annotation queues and error analysis that turn reviewer feedback into
  failure modes and metric suggestions
- Dataset ingestion that adds production traces, spans, or threads to
  datasets as goldens
- Python and TypeScript SDKs, OpenTelemetry for other languages, and an
  OpenInference integration

### Getting started

Create a free account at
[cloud.confident-ai.com](https://cloud.confident-ai.com), install
[`confident-trace`](https://github.com/confident-ai/confident-trace), Confident
AI's OpenTelemetry-native tracing SDK for Python and TypeScript, and set your
project API key:

```bash
pip install confident-trace
export CONFIDENT_API_KEY="your-api-key"
```

Then call `init()` once when your app starts. It instruments the providers and
frameworks you have installed, such as OpenAI and LangChain, so each LLM call
shows up as a trace without further code changes:

```python
from confident_trace import init

init()
```

Apps already instrumented with OpenTelemetry can send traces to Confident AI
in any language by pointing their OTLP exporter at its endpoint, although
Confident AI still recommends `confident-trace` for its built-in integrations.

## 2. LangWatch

[LangWatch](https://langwatch.ai) combines tracing with evaluation and agent
testing in one open-core platform. On the evaluation side, it runs experiments
on datasets and monitors that evaluate production traffic, drawing on a
library of evaluators that includes safety checks such as PII detection,
secret detection, and prompt injection screening. Thread-level monitors wait
until a conversation has been idle for a set time before scoring it as a
whole, and automations on monitor results can send an email, a Slack message,
or a webhook when a check fails. A visual Workflows editor lets you build
evaluation pipelines from prompts, code, HTTP calls, and evaluators and run
them on datasets.

A distinctive part of LangWatch is that its guardrails reuse the same
evaluators at runtime to block or redact a response before it reaches
the user, and its Agent Testing feature runs multi-turn conversations against
your agent with a simulated user. LangWatch also ships an AI gateway with a single
OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible endpoint for routing model calls.

### Key features

- Evaluator library covering quality and safety, plus custom LLM-as-a-judge
  evaluators
- Production monitors at trace and thread level, with alerts through email,
  Slack, or webhooks
- A visual Workflows editor for evaluation pipelines
- Guardrails that block or redact responses using PII and jailbreak detection
- Simulated multi-turn agent tests before release
- AI gateway with an OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible endpoint
- Self-hostable Apache-2.0 core, with enterprise features under a separate
  license

### Pricing

| Developer  | €0                   | 2 users, 50K events/month, 14 days of data                                 |
| Growth     | €29 per core seat/month | 200K events/month, then €5 per 100K, unlimited lite users, 30-day retention |

LangWatch charges for core seats and lets you add lite users at no extra cost.

- You want guardrails and evaluation to share the same evaluators
- You test agents by simulating multi-turn conversations
- An Apache-2.0 core you can self-host matters to you

## 3. Pydantic Logfire

[Pydantic Logfire](https://pydantic.dev/logfire) comes from the team behind
Pydantic and Pydantic AI. Because it's built on OpenTelemetry, LLM and tool
calls show up in the same trace as the HTTP requests and database queries
around them, which makes it easier to tell a model problem apart from an
application or infrastructure problem.

You query your data with standard SQL and run evaluations alongside your
traces, and a run that fails in Live View can be saved to a hosted dataset
with "Add to Dataset". Reviewers can also grade runs with Pass, Neutral, or
Fail verdicts through run annotations, which are still in beta. Logfire also includes an AI gateway for
routing model calls, though the overall workflow assumes engineers who are
comfortable writing SQL and their own evaluators, so it suits engineering-led teams better
than teams that want a large preset metric library.

### Key features

- OpenTelemetry-based traces covering LLM calls and the application around
  them
- Open-source SDKs for Python, TypeScript, and Rust under the MIT license
- Standard SQL over your telemetry, which also works with existing BI tools
- Hosted datasets built from production traces with "Add to Dataset"
- Run annotations (beta) for human review, plus an AI gateway

### Pricing

- Your engineers prefer SQL to a proprietary query language
- You already use Pydantic or Pydantic AI

## 4. OpenObserve

[OpenObserve](https://openobserve.ai) is an OpenTelemetry-native observability
backend that keeps logs, metrics, traces, and LLM spans in one system. For LLM
workloads, it tracks cost, tokens, latency, and errors, and lets you replay
agent sessions turn by turn.

Its online evaluations score live traces with LLM judges or remote scorers at
the span, trace, or session level. They're available in OpenObserve Cloud and
the Enterprise edition, and the scores land in a stream you can query like the
rest of your data. OpenObserve also covers review queues, prompt versioning, a
playground, and offline experiments on datasets, which makes it a reasonable
choice for infrastructure teams that want one backend for everything.

### Key features

- LLM spans stored next to logs, metrics, and distributed traces
- Cost, token, latency, and error tracking, with session replay
- Online evaluations with LLM judges or remote scorers (Cloud and Enterprise)
- Review queues, prompt management, a playground, and dataset experiments
- An MCP integration for querying observability data from AI agents and IDEs

## 5. Latitude

[Latitude](https://latitude.so) focuses on what happens after a failure shows
up in production. It runs 14 built-in flaggers that detect problems such as
user frustration, jailbreak attempts, refusals, PII leakage, and tool call
errors, groups similar failing traces into signals, and alerts you by email or
Slack when a signal appears or gets worse. Its Evaluations feature scores
completed sessions with conditions, an LLM judge, or a custom script, and
reviewers can annotate any session with a verdict and feedback.

According to Latitude's homepage, it can also hand a problem to Claude Code or
Cursor with the full context, and the coding agent opens a pull request with a
proposed fix for a human to review. Latitude is open source under the MIT
license and works with existing OpenTelemetry pipelines.

### Key features

- Similar failing traces grouped into signals
- 14 built-in flaggers covering user signals, agent behavior, cost, and
  response validity
- Evaluations that score completed sessions with conditions, an LLM judge, or
  a script
- Human annotations on sessions, plus semantic search across traces
- Email and Slack alerts when a signal appears or escalates
- Handoff to Claude Code or Cursor, which opens a fix PR
- OpenTelemetry-compatible ingestion, MIT license

### Pricing

| Plan       | Price     | Includes                                                         |
| ---------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Starter    | $0        | Unlimited seats, 20K credits/month, 30-day retention             |
| Pro        | $99/month | Unlimited seats, 100K credits/month, then $20 per 10K, 90-day retention |
| Enterprise | Custom    | Custom credits and retention, on-prem or custom cloud            |

Latitude measures usage in credits rather than traces, so check how your
volume maps to credits before choosing a plan.

**Latitude is for you if...**

- You want production failures grouped into signals and handed to your coding
  agent
- An MIT-licensed tool is important to your team

## 7. Honeycomb

[Honeycomb](https://www.honeycomb.io) is a general observability platform built
on distributed tracing and high-cardinality event data. Because it ingests
OpenTelemetry natively, LLM and tool calls appear in the same traces as the
HTTP requests and database queries around them, and you can slice them by
user, prompt version, model, or any other field you send.

Its Agent Timeline shows an agent conversation in sequence, with prompts, tool
calls, and failures in order, and a "Show Failures Only" option narrows it to
the failing tool calls. Honeycomb doesn't run LLM evaluations itself, so any
quality scores would have to come from another tool and be sent in as span
attributes.

### Key features

- Agent Timeline for multi-agent conversations, available to all customers
- Agent spans alongside API calls, database queries, and infrastructure
- High-cardinality queries on any field
- BubbleUp for finding what separates outlier requests from healthy ones

## 8. Langfuse

## 9. LangSmith

[LangSmith](https://smith.langchain.com) is the managed platform from the
LangChain team, combining tracing, evaluation, and prompt management. Its
traces show the full execution tree of an agent, including tool selections,
retrieved documents, and the exact parameters at every step, and it works with
any framework, although it goes deepest with LangChain and LangGraph.

LangSmith offers 30+ evaluator templates for safety, response quality,
trajectory, user behavior, and multimodal checks that work both online and
offline, along with multi-turn evaluators that score a whole conversation
thread once it goes idle. Automation rules can send traces to an annotation
queue or a dataset, or call a webhook when an evaluator gives a low score, and
alerts can fire when a feedback or online evaluation score crosses a threshold
over a 5 or 15 minute window, notifying Slack, PagerDuty, or a webhook.
Annotation queues let subject matter experts review and label traces, and that
feedback flows back into datasets.

### Key features

- Full execution-tree tracing for agents
- 30+ LLM-as-a-judge evaluator templates, plus thread-level evaluators for
  multi-turn conversations
- Automation rules that add traces to datasets or annotation queues, or
  trigger webhooks

## 10. Datadog LLM Observability

## Which LLM observability tool should you choose?

### Step 1: Decide how much evaluation you need in production

If latency, token usage, and errors are all you need, your existing APM or any
tracing tool on this list will do the job. If you need to know whether outputs
were good, you need evaluation running on production traffic. Every tool here
can do that in some form except Honeycomb and the open-source edition of
OpenObserve, whose online evaluations need Cloud or Enterprise, but they
differ a lot in how much you have to build yourself. If you want evaluation
and observability together with the least setup, Confident AI offers one of
the best combinations of the two, with 50+ metrics that run on production
traces out of the box. Among the others, LangWatch has an evaluator library,
LangSmith, Datadog, and Arize offer judge templates, Latitude ships built-in
flaggers, and Langfuse and Logfire expect you to write or configure most
evaluators.

### Step 2: Match the tool to your stack

- **Mixed or changing stacks:** Confident AI's `confident-trace` instruments
  OpenAI, LangChain, LangGraph, LlamaIndex, CrewAI, Pydantic AI, and the Vercel
  AI SDK, among others, and any OpenTelemetry app can send traces to it, so
  the same setup works across teams on different frameworks.
- **LangChain or LangGraph:** LangSmith has the tightest integration.
- **Full-stack context:** Pydantic Logfire and Honeycomb put agent spans next
  to the services and queries around them.
- **Already on Datadog:** Datadog LLM Observability covers tracing, judge
  templates, and monitors without a new vendor.
- **One backend for all telemetry:** OpenObserve stores LLM spans with your
  logs and metrics.

### Step 3: Decide who owns AI quality

Any tool on this list can work when only engineers look at traces, but once
PMs, QA, and domain experts need to take part, it's worth looking at how far
each tool goes. Langfuse,
LangSmith, Datadog, OpenObserve, and Arize offer annotation or review queues,
Latitude lets reviewers annotate sessions, and LangWatch includes unlimited
lite users on its paid plan along with a visual Workflows editor for building
evaluation pipelines. In our view, Confident AI goes furthest here, because
reviewers can work through annotation queues and also run evaluations against
your app over HTTP without writing code.

### Step 4: Check the constraint that decides it

- **Self-hosting on a free plan:** Langfuse (MIT), Latitude (MIT), LangWatch
  (Apache-2.0 core), and OpenObserve (AGPL-3.0) are open source. Arize Phoenix
  is free to self-host under the source-available Elastic License 2.0.
- **Turning failures into fixes:** Latitude hands signals to your coding agent,
  and Confident AI turns reviewer feedback into failure modes and metrics.
- **Cost at scale:** pricing units differ across vendors, including GB-months
  (Confident AI), GB ingested and queried (OpenObserve), events (Honeycomb,
  LangWatch), spans (Datadog, Arize), records (Logfire), units (Langfuse),
  credits (Latitude), and seats plus traces (LangSmith), so estimate your own
  volume against each one.
